# Retention, recovery and rollout — unfinished, no production use

The current production collector candidate deletes events/evidence after 7 days and can remove historical HMAC keys when no evidence refers to them. This notice candidate's read-time evidence verification requires the original event, evidence and signing key. Without coordination, the existing purge would make the notice list fail integrity checks. The staged SQL patches the exact purge selection to preserve all notice-referenced evidence and retains its keys; unreferenced events still follow 7 days. It caps notice rows at 1,000 and records a persistent capacity-gap count; actions are bounded per event. This is only an isolated candidate: held evidence cannot yet be archived/unpinned, capacity-gap monitoring and recovery are not wired, and the patched definition still needs an exact production catalog guard. **Do not deploy or enable it as-is.** Neither the old retention audit nor the candidate's audit table is an independently anchored immutable record.

Before deployment, root must design and test together, in a single owned DB lane:

1. Choose a formally approved retention period and payload minimization policy. Map the existing 7-day event/evidence, 90-day reader audit and 365-day retention audit windows to notice/review and legally appropriate retention; no automatic deletion of unresolved notices. No assumption that audit retention may be unlimited.
2. Produce a cryptographically verified, minimized, independently anchored archive receipt before any source/key removal. Preserve event identity, original signed evidence, trusted key version, verified canonical digest, operator/actions/versions/times and retention rationale without retaining raw network traffic or new personal data. Independent anchor authority must be separate from collector's failure domain; same-database hashes alone do not stop privileged rollback.
3. Atomically coordinate bounded prune of collector evidence, keys, notice inbox/outbox and audit. No orphaned notices; unknown key, anchor unavailability, drift or active review fails closed with an operational alert and no partial deletion. Native PG17 concurrent ingress/read/retention and restart/restore tests must validate safe ordering. The archive and event bounds must report coverage loss rather than silently dropping new or held records.
4. Model OFF intervals, capacity exhaustion, downstream downtime, collector timestamps, expired keys and backlog recovery. Establish bounded backfill from valid original signed evidence without retiming or inventing observations; old evidence outside validation/retention limits stays explicitly held. Capture separate indicators for observation, queued, inbox available, displayed, read and reviewed.
5. The one-use read proof and immutable command ID allow a lost response to be safely retried only under the same actor and live SID. If the session changes, do not repeat the old operation: the authenticated Master must open the authoritative list with a fresh proof and inspect the result. No invented success; failed, unknown or changed-account operations remain unresolved.
6. Prepare and independently verify installation/catalog/ACL guards, default OFF, exact dependencies, unused-only rollback, forward recovery after use, backup restore and separate key/recovery custody. Stop traffic and preserve evidence on any rollback mismatch. No user/company data, production keys, notification recipients or public endpoints in fixtures.
7. Only after physical host, monitored keys, delivery mechanism, reliable notifications, anchor and runbooks exist, exercise normal/unauthorized access, notification delivery/display, error recovery, false-positive handling and owner iPhone. Real third-party notifications require explicit authorization.
